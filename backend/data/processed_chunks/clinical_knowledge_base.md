### CCRAS Standardized Prakriti Assessment: Physical & Anatomical Predictors — Core Classical Clinical Concept
**Parent Guideline:** CCRAS Standardized Prakriti Assessment: Physical & Anatomical Predictors | **Domain:** prakriti_assessment

According to the classical Ayurvedic treatises (*Charaka Samhita*, *Sushruta Samhita*, and *Ashtanga Hridaya*), physical and anatomical predictors (*Sharirika Lakshana*) serve as objective markers for determining an individual's baseline constitutional phenotype (*Deha Prakriti*). These phenotypic characteristics originate during fertilization (*Garbhana-kale*) based on the dominance of *Tridosha* and remain largely immutable throughout life. Physical predictors encompass structural frame (*Asthi-Mamsa Sarata*), integumentary parameters (*Twak/Loma/Kesha*), ocular features (*Netra Lakshana*), dental features (*Danta*), and articular integrity (*Sandhi-Bandhana*).

Standardization by the Central Council for Research in Ayurvedic Sciences (CCRAS) translates these classical concepts into standardized diagnostic predictors to enable reproducible clinical scoring across diagnostic frameworks.

Separating baseline constitutional physiological traits (*Prakriti*) from transient acquired metabolic derangements (*Vikriti*) is critical for automated digital diagnostic scoring.

### WHO Benchmark: Panchakarma Procedures, Indications & Strict Contraindications — 1. International Benchmark Principles
**Parent Guideline:** WHO Benchmark: Panchakarma Procedures, Indications & Strict Contraindications | **Domain:** ayurveda

* **Definition of Pañcakarma**: A comprehensive therapeutic system consisting of five main cleansing methodologies (*pradhāna karma*) designed to prepare, physically expel, or remove vitiated *doṣa* (biological factors) and *mala* (excretable materials), thereby re-establishing normal metabolic function (*agni*).
* **Classification of Procedures**:
 * **Pūrva Karma (Preparatory Procedures)**: Interventions that prepare the body for main cleansing, including *accha-snehapāna* (administration of lipid-based medicines in titrated quantities) and *svedana* (application of heat).
 * **Pradhāna Karma (Main Procedures)**: The five primary therapeutic modalities:
 1. *Vamana*: Therapeutically induced emesis.
 2. *Virecana*: Therapeutically induced purgation.
 3. *Nasya*: Nasal administration of medicaments.
 4. *Āsthāpanavasti / Kaṣāyavasti / Nirūhavasti*: Medicated enemas composed predominantly of herbal decoctions.
 5. *Anuvāsanavasti / Snehavasti*: Medicated enemas utilizing lipid-based preparations (oils/ghee).
 * **Paścāt Karma (Post-Therapy Procedures)**: Post-cleansing measures, notably *saṃsarjana* (a systematic, controlled dietary and activity regimen) aimed at restoring gastrointestinal digestive power (*jaṭharāgni*).
* **Practice Level Requirements**: Complex *pañcakarma* interventions—such as titrated *accha-snehapāna*, customized liquid enema combinations, and potent agents like *tuvarakarasāyana* (*Hydnocarpus laurifolia*)—are restricted to advanced or specialty-level practice (Type II practitioners) and require appropriate facility infrastructure.

### WHO Benchmark: Ayurvedic Diagnostic Assessment Principles (Trividha, Ashtavidha Pariksha) — 1. International Benchmark Principles
**Parent Guideline:** WHO Benchmark: Ayurvedic Diagnostic Assessment Principles (Trividha, Ashtavidha Pariksha) | **Domain:** ayurveda

According to WHO benchmarks, Ayurvedic diagnostic assessment relies on a comprehensive, systematic framework to evaluate body physiology, pathology, and patient status. The primary objective of diagnostic assessment is two-fold: maintaining/promoting health in the healthy (*svāsthyasyorjaskaraṃ*) and treating pathology in the unwell (*ārtasyaroganut*).

* **Foundational Etiopathological Framework (*Nidāna Pañcaka*)**:
 * **Nidāna**: Identification of causative and precipitating factors.
 * **Pūrvarūpa**: Recognition of early prodromal signs and symptoms.
 * **Rūpa**: Analysis of the full spectrum of manifest clinical signs and symptoms.
 * **Upaśaya & Anupaśaya**: Diagnostic observation of positive or negative patient responses to specific exploratory interventions (therapeutic trials) to deduce obscure disease pathology.
 * **Saṃprāpti**: Mapping the precise pathogenesis and disease pathway from causative exposure to manifest tissue imbalance.
* **Physiological Triad Assessment (*Doṣa-Dhātu-Mala*)**:
 * **Doṣa**: Evaluating functional biological balance—*Vāta* (motion), *Pitta* (transformation), and *Kapha* (cumulation)—and their synchronized interplay among the five basic elements (*Pañca-mahābhūta*).
 * **Dhātu**: Assessing the functional and structural integrity of the body's structural elements (*dhātu*).
 * **Mala**: Evaluating the elimination and metabolic balance of bodily excretions (*mala*).
* **Diagnostic Examination Methods**:
 * **Trividha Parīkṣā (Threefold Examination)**: *Darśana* (inspection/observation), *Sparśana* (palpation/touch), and *Praśna* (interrogation/detailed history taking).
 * **Aṣṭavidha Parīkṣā (Eightfold Examination)**: Diagnostic evaluation encompassing *Nāḍī* (pulse), *Mūtra* (urine), *Purīṣa* (stool), *Jihvā* (tongue), *Śabda* (voice/body sounds), *Sparśa* (skin/touch), *Dṛk* (eyes/vision), and *Ākṛti* (general bodily frame/physiognomy).

 - **Monitoring:** Serum electrolytes, creatinine, and uric acid.

2. **Enalapril (ACE Inhibitor)**
 - **Dose:** 2.5 mg to 10 mg PO twice daily.
 - **Initiation Criteria:** SBP > 100 mmHg, normal electrolytes, and serum creatinine < 2.5 mg/dL.
 - **Titration:** Uptitrate every 1–2 weeks to the maximum tolerable dose.
 - **Monitoring:** Check BP and electrolytes before each dose increment and during follow-up.

3. **Spironolactone (MRA)**
 - **Dose:** 25–50 mg PO once daily.
 - **Monitoring:** Check serum potassium and creatinine every 2–4 weeks.

4. **Carvedilol (Beta-Blocker)**
 - **Dose:** 3.125 mg to 25 mg PO twice daily.
 - **Initiation Criteria:** Start **ONLY after complete decongestion**, provided SBP > 100 mmHg and HR > 60/min.
 - **Titration:** Uptitrate every 1–2 weeks to maximum tolerable dose.
 - **Monitoring:** Watch BP, heart rate, and potential precipitation of heart failure symptoms.
 - **Managing CHF Recurrence:** Increase diuretic dose and temporarily reduce carvedilol.

### ICMR Protocol: Management of Chronic Heart Failure (CHF) — Level-wise Referral Protocol
**Parent Guideline:** ICMR Protocol: Management of Chronic Heart Failure (CHF) | **Domain:** allopathy

#### **1. Primary Health Centre (PHC)**
- Differentiate CHF from respiratory conditions (e.g., breathlessness with fever/cough/expectoration or known asthma/COPD).
- If CHF is likely: Initiating decongestion with Furosemide.
- Refer immediately if Red Flag emergency indicators are present.

#### **2. Community Health Centre (CHC)**
- Admit and stabilize.
- Send basic investigations (ECG to rule out STEMI; Chest X-ray to exclude primary respiratory etiology).
- Decongest with IV Furosemide; administer O2 therapy if SpO2 < 90%.
- Initiate oral Enalapril and Spironolactone.
- Consider Carvedilol **after** clinical decongestion.

#### **3. District Hospital**
- Admit, re-assess, and optimize medical therapy (Furosemide, Enalapril, Spironolactone, O2).
- Provide Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV) for marked respiratory distress with SpO2 < 90%.
- Perform 2D Echocardiography to confirm HFrEF (LVEF < 35%).
- Investigate underlying etiology; initiate Carvedilol post-decongestion.
- Refer back to CHC/PHC once stabilized.

#### **4. Tertiary Care Centre**
- **Indications for Referral:** Uncontrolled CHF, unstable hemodynamics, suspected ongoing ischemia, abnormal electrolytes/renal parameters, structural heart disease, or unclear etiology.
- **Management:**
 - Categorize HF: Acute (<3 months) vs Chronic (>3 months); HFrEF (<35%) vs HFpEF (35–50%).
 - Advanced Medical Therapy: Consider ARNI (Sacubitril/Valsartan) and Ivabradine.
 - Preventive Care: Pneumococcal and Influenza vaccinations.
 - Invasive / Non-Pharmacological Interventions:
 - **ICD:** Implantable Cardioverter Defibrillator in selected patients.
 - **CRT / BiV:** Cardiac Resynchronization Therapy if NYHA class II/III, LVEF < 35%, QRS > 150 ms with LBBB morphology on optimal medical therapy > 3 months.
 - **Etiology-Based Procedures:** PCI, CABG, or Valve Replacement.

### ICMR Protocol: Management of Stable Angina — Immediate Red Flags & Emergency Triage Indicators
**Parent Guideline:** ICMR Protocol: Management of Stable Angina | **Domain:** allopathy

#### **1. Rule Out Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS) - Immediate Referral to Emergency/STEMI Protocol:**
* Angina presenting at rest or lasting **> 20 minutes**.
* **Crescendo Angina:** Recent worsening of stable angina to CCS Class III.
* **New-Onset Effort Angina:** Less than 1 month duration in CCS Class II/III.
* **Post-Infarction Angina.**

#### **2. Clinical Features Supporting Angina:**
* Diffuse retrosternal pain, heaviness, constriction with radiation to arms, neck, or back.
* Accompanied by profuse sweating.
* Easily reproduced by post-meal exertion.
* *Atypical Presentations:* Exertional fatigue, breathlessness, profuse sweating, or epigastric discomfort.
* Higher likelihood in patients with known Coronary Artery Disease (CAD).

#### **3. Features Making Angina Unlikely:**
* Variable location or characteristics.
* Duration long (hours to days) or very short (< 1 minute).
* Restricted to areas strictly above the jaw or below the epigastrium.
* Pain localized to a single point; pricking, piercing, or stabbing type.
* Pain precipitated by neck/arm movement or respiration.

### ICMR Protocol: Management of Stable Angina — Level-Wise Referral Protocol
**Parent Guideline:** ICMR Protocol: Management of Stable Angina | **Domain:** allopathy

#### **PHC / CHC Level**
1. Initiate angina symptom control using **Metoprolol**; add **Nitrates** if symptoms persist.
2. Obtain 12-lead ECG (evaluate for Q waves, ST-T changes, Bundle Branch Blocks, or chamber enlargement).
3. Initiate **Aspirin** and **high-intensity Statin** therapy.
4. Refer electively to a higher center.

#### **District Hospital Level**
1. Optimize anti-anginal medical therapy.
2. Perform Echocardiography to evaluate LV function and structural heart disease.
3. Perform Risk Stratification via Exercise Treadmill Test (DUKE Risk Score) for ambulatory patients with interpretable baseline ECGs.
4. **Refer to Tertiary Centre if:**
 * Angina remains uncontrolled on Optimal Medical Therapy (OMT).
 * Echo reveals structural/LV abnormalities.
 * Patient is non-ambulatory or baseline ECG is uninterpretable.
 * High risk determined on exercise stress testing.

#### **Tertiary Level**
1. Reassess and optimize multi-drug anti-anginal regimen (incorporate Trimetazidine, Nicorandil, Ranolazine, or Ivabradine as required).
2. Perform Stress Imaging (Nuclear/Echo) if patient is non-ambulatory, baseline ECG is abnormal/uninterpretable, ETT result is equivocal, or LV function is compromised.
3. **Revascularization Workup:**
 * **Low/Intermediate Risk:** Continue Optimal Medical Therapy with follow-up every 3–6 months at primary/secondary care.
 * **High Risk:** Perform Coronary Angiography if angina is uncontrolled on OMT, non-invasive testing shows high risk, or patient is a cardiac arrest survivor/has documented VT. Revascularize if coronary anatomy is suitable.
